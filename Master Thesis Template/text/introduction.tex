\chapter{Introduction}

\section*{Context and Motivation}

The transformation of video games from a niche hobby to a dominant force in the entertainment industry has fundamentally reshaped how individuals interact, compete, and even build careers. In this new era of \textbf{competitive gaming}, players engage in high-stakes environments that reward precision, strategy, and reflexes. Fueled by global connectivity and high-speed internet, online multiplayer games have evolved into structured \textbf{competitive ecosystems}—complete with rankings, seasonal events, and financial incentives—that mirror traditional sports in both scope and intensity.

Video games now serve not only as entertainment, but also as \textbf{social platforms, economic drivers, and professional arenas}. Yet, this rapid evolution has also given rise to a persistent and multifaceted threat: \textbf{cheating}. By leveraging unauthorized software modifications, players seek unfair advantages that distort the competitive balance. These exploits range from memory manipulation and external assistance tools to hardware-based hacks and network-level abuses. The resulting \textbf{arms race} between developers—who continually enhance anti-cheat mechanisms—and cheat creators—who innovate to bypass them—has become one of the most dynamic and technically challenging conflicts in the digital age.

Furthermore, the deployment of modern anti-cheat systems introduces complex \textbf{privacy, legal, and environmental concerns}. Effective cheat detection often requires invasive system-level access, raising red flags regarding user privacy and digital rights. Simultaneously, the commercialization of cheating software generates substantial financial losses for developers and presents regulatory challenges on a global scale. Additionally, the increased computational demand of sophisticated detection systems contributes to a growing environmental footprint.

To move beyond theoretical discourse, this thesis adopts a hands-on approach: the implementation and analysis of a custom-built cheat in a large-scale multiplayer game. This methodology enables a detailed examination of anti-cheat technologies from a practical perspective, offering empirical insights into their design, effectiveness, and limitations.

By combining historical context, technical exploration, and real-world experimentation, this research aims to contribute a comprehensive understanding of cheating in competitive gaming—its motivations, its mechanisms, and its broader societal implications.

\section*{Objectives and Scope}

This study seeks to investigate cheating in competitive video games through both theoretical analysis and practical experimentation. The research is structured around three main objectives:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{To examine the historical evolution of cheating in gaming}, analyzing its impact on game design, player behavior, and industry responses.
    
    \item \textbf{To provide a technical breakdown of common cheating techniques}, such as aimbots, wallhacks, memory injection, and network manipulation, along with their consequences for competitive fairness.
    
    \item \textbf{To implement and evaluate a custom cheat in a contemporary multiplayer game}, assessing the robustness of current anti-cheat technologies through empirical testing and reverse engineering.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Document Structure}

This thesis is organized as follows:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Chapter 1: Introduction} – Outlines the motivation, objectives, competencies, and structure of the study.
    
    \item \textbf{Chapter 2: From Primitive Survival to Digital Domination} – Investigates the human instinct for competition and its evolution into virtual arenas, tracing the technological milestones that gave rise to competitive gaming.
    
    \item \textbf{Chapter 3: The Evolution of Cheating: From Debugging Tools to an Organized Industry} – Explores the origins of game cheating, its progression into an underground economy, and the industry’s adaptive response via anti-cheat systems.
    
    \item \textbf{Chapter 4: The Prevalence and Impact of Cheating in Online Multiplayer Games} – Analyzes the consequences of cheating on game balance, player trust, and industry economics, with a focus on Counter-Strike 2 as a case study.
    
    \item \textbf{Chapter 5: Injection and Detection Techniques} – Provides a technical overview of code injection methods, detection strategies, and the ongoing battle between cheaters and anti-cheat developers.
    
    \item \textbf{Chapter 6: Internal Game Structure: Logic Behind Counter-Strike 2} – Dissects the architecture of the CS2 engine, including memory layouts, entity management, and reverse engineering methods.
    
    \item \textbf{Chapter 7: Cheat Features Implemented in Our Cheat for CS2} – Presents the design and implementation of various cheat modules, including visibility enhancements, automated aiming, and anti-detection features.
\end{itemize}
